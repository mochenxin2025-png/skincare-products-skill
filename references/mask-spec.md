# Mask Specification v1.0

Supplement to the main framework. Covers wash-off clay/mud masks, sheet and hydrogel masks, and sleeping masks, and defines the category boundaries against Exfoliation and Moisturizer. Load this when the product category is identified as **"Mask"**.

---

## 1. First Principle

A mask's only real mechanism advantage is **contact time × occlusion**. It holds product against the skin, or seals the skin, for minutes at a time — which raises the delivered dose of whatever the formula contains above what an ordinary leave-on can reach.

That advantage is symmetric. **Occlusion amplifies penetration of actives and of irritants, sensitisers, and preservatives alike.** A mask that looks well-formulated on paper can be a much harsher product in use than the same formula in a bottle.

A mask should NOT be interpreted as:
- "Hydrating the skin from within" — the measurable effect is stratum corneum water content, which peaks during use and falls back within hours
- "Detoxifying" or "drawing out impurities" (clay adsorbs surface sebum; it does not extract anything from the dermis)
- "Feeding" the skin, or delivering nutrients past the barrier
- A daily necessity — frequency is a barrier-load decision, not a default

---

## 2. Four Format Classes

Classify by format first. The format determines the contact mode, the dose, and the risk.

| Format | Contact | Mechanism | Handling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Wash-off clay / mud** (kaolin, bentonite, mud, charcoal) | 10–20 min, rinsed | Adsorption of surface sebum; light mechanical removal on rinse | Score in this spec. Adsorbent ingredients are `[FS]`, not actives. |
| **Sheet / hydrogel** (non-woven, hydrogel, bio-cellulose, soaked cotton) | 15–30 min, often left on | **Occlusion-enhanced penetration** — the signature mechanism | Score in this spec. Actives get the occlusion uplift (§4); irritants get the matching penalty (§6). |
| **Sleeping / leave-on mask** | Hours | Film-forming moisturisation; functionally a heavy moisturiser | **Route to Moisturizer** — do not score as a mask |
| **Peel-off film mask** | 15–30 min, peeled | Physical removal of corneocytes | **Route to Exfoliation** |

Format ambiguity is common ("overnight clay", "peel-off mud"). Resolve by what the user does with it: **rinsed, peeled, or left on.** If the instructions are not available, ask — the three outcomes differ by a whole weight profile.

## 4. Core Effectiveness Scoring (Mask)

The contact mode sets the bar. Apply the row that matches the format.

| Format | Level 0.9–1.0 | Level 0.5–0.69 | Level 0.0–0.29 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sheet / hydrogel | A clinical active at an effective concentration, in a formula whose occlusion is used deliberately, with a supporting solvent system | A few humectants and a trace botanical; the "efficacy" is water held against the skin | Water + fragrance; no active above trace; all claims are sensory |
| Wash-off clay | A genuinely adsorbent clay at a functional position, with a formulated (not drying) base | Clay present at a low position; base is mostly water and thickener | "Detox" claims; charcoal as decoration; clay listed after the preservative |
| Sleeping | — | — | — (route to Moisturizer) |

Mandatory:

- **For wash-off formats, contact time caps the effect.** Ingredients in the last third of the list are trace → `[M]`, per the framework's standing rinse-off rule. A clay mask cannot be a "treatment" for a deep skin concern.
- **For sheet masks, the uplift is real but bounded.** Do not credit a sheet mask with clinical efficacy the active is not known to have. Occlusion raises delivered dose; it does not create a mechanism.
- **A mask is a single short exposure, not a routine.** Do not average its actives with the rest of a routine to reach an efficacy conclusion.
- The liquid a sheet mask carries is typically 20–30 g held for 15–30 minutes — evaluate it as a **short, high-dose exposure**, not as a light mist.

## 5. Architecture (Weight 25)

| Element | Evaluate |
| --- | --- |
| Format material | Non-woven (basic), hydrogel (better adhesion and occlusion), bio-cellulose (best conformity); clay bases (adsorbent capacity, dryness of the base) |
| Occlusion quality | Full seal vs partial contact — a loose sheet delivers far less |
| Liquid load | Volume and viscosity; too thin → drips and the contact fails |
| Solvent support | Glycols and solubilisers decide whether a lipophilic active is delivered at all |
| Clay base quality | Kaolin/bentonite with glycerin and oils vs clay + water + drying alcohol (the latter strips) |
| Preservative system | Water-based, frequently single-preservative, sometimes none — see §7 |
| Film formers | Legitimate for peel-off and sleeping formats; cosmetic otherwise |

---

## 6. The Occlusion Amplification Rule

This is the signature rule of the category. **Apply it every time a mask is occlusive** (sheet, hydrogel, sleeping):

| Formula contains | Uplift to | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A beneficial water-soluble active | Core Effectiveness | Raised penetration — the mechanism the format exists for |
| Fragrance / essential oils | Safety PS **−0.15** | 15–30 minutes of sealed contact is a far larger sensitiser dose than the same formula applied and left open |
| High-concentration ethanol | Safety PS **−0.15** | Sealed penetration of a solvent onto compromised skin |
| A potent preservative system (formaldehyde releasers, MIT/CMIT) | Safety PS **−0.15** | Prolonged occluded contact with a leave-on preservative |
| A single or absent preservative system | Flag | Water-based product with inadequate protection — and mask packaging multiplies the risk (§7) |

And the practical corollary, which belongs in the report: **if a sheet mask contains fragrance or a potent preservative, recommend rinsing after use** rather than leaving the residue on.

---

## 7. Hygiene and Microbiology (mask-specific)

No other category has this risk profile, because no other category holds a water-based product against the skin in this way.

| Packaging | Risk | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single-use sachet / individually wrapped sheet | Low | — |
| Multi-use jar, fingers or spatula | **Higher** | Flag whenever the preservative system is weak (single preservative, or none); the user opens the jar repeatedly and introduces water and flora |
| Reusable sheet mask / silicone cover | Highest | Flag; requires washing between uses |
| Clay/mud in a wide-mouth jar | Moderate | Flag if preservative coverage is thin |

State the preservation adequacy explicitly for jar-packaged masks. This is a food-safety-grade question applied to cosmetics: water + repeated opening + weak preservation is the classic failure mode.

## 8. Format-Specific Rules

**Wash-off clay**
- Over-adsorption: a strongly adsorbent clay left until it cracks pulls water with the sebum. Dry, sensitive, and barrier-damaged skin → ⚠️/❌; instruct to remove before the mask dries.
- Charcoal: no clinical evidence of adsorption benefit over clay → `[M]`.
- "Pore extraction" / "blackhead removal" claims → no mechanism for extraction; the visible effect is surface sebum removal and temporary swelling of the follicle opening. Flag.
- Clay position: if it appears after the preservative, the adsorbent system is decorative.

**Sheet / hydrogel**
- "Leave it on until it dries" is counter-productive: once the sheet dries it begins to draw water back. Flag any instruction implying extended wear.
- Residual essence: whether to rinse is a formula question, not a preference — see §6.
- "Medical-grade" / "医美面膜" claims → imply a medical action → flag.

**Sleeping masks**
- Functionally a heavy moisturiser with film formers. Route to Moisturizer; the mask spec does not score it.
- If a sleeping mask is marketed for acne or exfoliation, route by primary intent instead (Acne Serum / Exfoliation).

## 9. Skin Adaptation — Mask

| Skin type / condition | Suitability | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | ✅ | |
| Dry | ✅ / ⚠️ | Hydrating sheet masks are appropriate; drying clay is not |
| Oily | ✅ | Clay wash-off suits; sheet masks are acceptable |
| Combination | ✅ | Zone the clay to the T-zone |
| Sensitive | ⚠️ / ❌ | Fragrance-free sheet masks only; clay and peel-off unsuitable |
| Acne-prone | ⚠️ | Occlusion can occlude follicles; prefer clay wash-off over heavy sheet masks |
| Seborrheic dermatitis | ⚠️ / ❌ | Occlusion may worsen the condition; fragrance is a common trigger |
| Rosacea | ❌ / ⚠️ | Heat and occlusion trigger flushing |
| Barrier-damaged | ❌ | Occlusion over a damaged barrier increases penetration of everything, including irritants |
| Keratosis pilaris | ⚠️ | Not the right format; see Exfoliation |
| Body acne | ⚠️ | Large-area occlusion is not appropriate |
| Infant | ❌ | Not appropriate |

## 10. Category Boundary Rules

Precedence: `Exfoliation > Acne Serum > Mask > Toner > Moisturizer`

| Product | Category | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Peel-off film mask | **Exfoliation** | Physical removal of corneocytes |
| Sleeping / overnight mask | **Moisturizer** | No short-contact mechanism; a heavy leave-on |
| Compressed sheet soaked in an acid toner | **Exfoliation** if the intent is keratolysis, otherwise **Mask** |
| Clay mask marketed for acne lesion reduction | **Mask** if the mechanism is adsorption; **Acne Serum** if the formula is a leave-on treatment |
| Sheet mask soaked in a hydrating toner | **Mask** | Contact mode changes the physics; the vehicle alone would be a Toner |

---

## 11. Anti-Hallucination Rules (Mask)

- **"The mask hydrates the skin"** means stratum corneum water content rises during use and falls back within hours. Say that, not "it hydrates the dermis".
- **"Leave it on longer for more effect"** is false for wash-off and drying formats — beyond the intended contact time, the mask begins to draw water out. Flag any such instruction.
- **"Daily masking"** has no supporting evidence and multiplies exposure to fragrances and preservatives. Do not recommend it.
- **"Detox", "pore cleansing", "impurity extraction"** → no mechanism → flag as unsupported.
- **"Medical-grade" / "医美" / "post-procedure"** claims → imply medical action → flag.
- **Charcoal, gold, diamond, snail, bird's nest, stem cells** → `[M]` unless a mechanism and a concentration are declared.
- **Do not credit count.** Twenty botanicals in the last third of a sheet-mask liquid is marketing, and the framework's overuse penalty applies.
- **A mask's actives do not accumulate with the routine.** Score the product, and state that it is a single short exposure.
- **Peel-off and clay formats on barrier-damaged skin → ❌**, regardless of the ingredient list quality.

## 12. Ingredient Quick Reference

**Functional in a mask**
Kaolin · Bentonite · Glycerin · Butylene glycol · Panthenol · Allantoin · Sodium hyaluronate · Madecassoside (supportive) · Niacinamide (2–5%) · Ceramides (if delivery is formulated)

**Occlusion uplift applies — evaluate exposure, not just benefit**
Any clinical active in a sheet mask · Fragrance and essential oils (flag) · Ethanol (flag) · Potent preservative systems (flag)

**Decorative / marketing in this format**
Charcoal · Gold, diamond, pearl · Snail mucin, bird's nest, collagen · Ferments without declared metabolites · Most botanical blends in the last third

**Format markers**
Non-woven / hydrogel / bio-cellulose → sheet class · Kaolin/bentonite/mud → clay class · PVA/acrylic film formers → peel-off (→ Exfoliation) · Heavy occlusives + film formers only → sleeping (→ Moisturizer)
